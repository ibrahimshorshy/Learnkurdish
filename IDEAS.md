# Twelve things worth doing next

Not built. Ordered by how much each would help the account, not by size.

### 1. Back up and restore the library

Every recording, line and video lives in this browser's storage. Clearing site
data, switching browsers or moving to a new computer loses all of it. A
"Back up" button that writes one ZIP (recordings, takes, projects as JSON) and
a "Restore" that reads it back would protect the one thing that cannot be
regenerated: your voice.

### 2. Publish and schedule through Buffer

The captions and hashtags already exist for every video. Buffer's API can take
the MP4 and the caption and queue it for Instagram, TikTok and Facebook at your
posting times. That replaces "download → move to phone → retype caption" with
one click, and a daily series becomes realistic.

### 3. A series, numbered

Turn the first 30 posts into "Daily Kurdish 1/30 … 30/30": a series name, a part
number on the title card, and a closing card that says what comes next. Series
are what turn viewers into followers.

### 4. Word-by-word breakdown

After a line plays, show its words one by one with their meaning — "چۆنی = how
are you". The translation pass can return the gloss with the line. Viewers save
vocabulary reels far more than phrase reels.

### 5. Quiz reels from lessons you already made

Take any approved lesson and make a second video from it: show the Arabic or
English, pause three seconds, then play your Kurdish. No new recording, no new
AI cost. It doubles your output from the same work and gets comments.

### 6. Flashcards export (Anki / Quizlet)

Every line already has your voice clip, the Kurdish, the pronunciation and two
translations — exactly a flashcard. Export a deck per lesson as a free download
or a paid pack: a product from work already done.

### 7. Record on your phone, edit on the computer

Phone microphones are often better than laptop ones, and you may want to record
away from the desk. A QR code on screen that opens a simple recorder page on the
phone (same Wi-Fi) and sends the take straight into the project. The server
now answers only on this computer, so this would open one narrow door for the
phone (a single upload route with a one-time code in the QR) rather than
switching on `HOST=0.0.0.0` for everything.

### 8. Background music under the voice

A quiet loop that ducks whenever you speak. The mixdown already knows exactly
when speech happens. Silence during the "your turn" pause can read as a dead
video on a feed.

### 9. Square, 4:5 and 16:9

Feed posts and Facebook prefer 1:1 and 4:5; YouTube long-form wants 16:9 for
stories and dialogues. The renderer is resolution-independent, so this is
mostly new layout entries.

### 10. A native-speaker check before publishing

Send a review link to an Arabic or English proofreader (or a second Kurdish
teacher) who can comment on single lines. Wrong translations are the mistake
that damages trust, and the hardest to catch in a language you teach rather
than speak natively.

### 11. Reach Persian and Turkish speakers

Both are already in the app, switched off. Iran and Turkey have large audiences
interested in Kurdish. Turning them on doubles the videos per recording; they
only need a native speaker to check the on-video words once.

### 12. Learn from what performs

Buffer (or Instagram Insights) knows which reels were watched, saved and shared.
Pulling those numbers next to each project in the Library shows which topics,
lengths and formats work, so the next 30 posts are chosen by evidence.
